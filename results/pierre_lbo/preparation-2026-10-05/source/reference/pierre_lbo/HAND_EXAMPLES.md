## Hand calculations

The common example has annual revenue 1464, gross profit 732, opex 366 and EBITDA 366. Entry term debt is 100, so entry equity is 266. Half is preferred (133), half ordinary (133), with management owning 20% of ordinary: management invests 26.6 and sponsor 239.4. The fiscal year runs from 1 December 2023 to 1 December 2024 (366 days); closing is 2 December 2023, leaving 365 owned days. Cash EBITDA and baseline capex are each 365, but annual valuation EBITDA remains 366. Interest and preferred accrual use exactly one ACT/365 year.

1. **Repayment ordering:** change first annual capex to 466 and second to zero, exit in period two, and sweep all cash. First draw is `D = 100*365/366`. Second-period EBITDA is 366 and opening-revolver interest is `D/10`. Repay revolver principal `D` before sweeping the remaining term principal 100. Cash left is `366 - 1.1*D - 100`. The initial draw has no first-period revolver interest. This arithmetic does not depend on the model's computed balances.
2. **Failure and retained prefix:** retain first annual capex 366, set second capex to 500, commitment to 10 and exit to period three. First-period net cash is zero. Second-period deficit is `500 - 366 = 134`; shortage is `134 - 10 = 124`. Exactly one financing row survives, all three forecast dates survive, planned exit is 1 December 2026, and every exit/waterfall/return field is null.
3. **Preferred priority:** set exit EV to 232, 233 or 234 by dividing each by annual EBITDA 366 to get its multiple. After debt100, proceeds are 132, 133 or 134. Preferred receives 132, 133 or 133; ordinary receives 0, 0 or 1. Management receives 0, 0 or 0.2. Its first two IRRs are null; the third is `0.2/26.6 - 1 = 1/133 - 1`, because the actual holding period is one year. Total distributions exactly conserve proceeds apart from binary64 arithmetic.
4. **Dated returns:** close on 29 February 2024, exit on 1 December 2024, and use exit multiple2. The hold is 276 days. EBITDA and capex still cancel; proceeds are `2*366 - 100 = 632`. Deal MoM is `632/266`, IRR is `(632/266)^(365/276)-1`, preferred is133 and ordinary499. Integer-year CAGR is not this result.
5. **A real, small liquidity deficit:** baseline cash EBITDA/capex cancel; working capital consumes `10 + 5e-9` with commitment10. The economic shortage is `5e-9`. Multiply every monetary input by 0.001, 1 or 1000: shortages become `5e-12`, `5e-9`, `5e-6`. All are above the new row guard (`365e-12*scale`), so all fail. The former absolute1e-8 rule classified them ok, ok, fail. Recorded before/after values are in `runs/private/pierre_lbo/audit-financial-evidence.json`.

## Numerical policy and neighboring operations

Liquidity uses `1e-12 * max(opening_cash, abs(cash_ebitda), cash_capex, abs(change_working_capital), cash_taxes, cash_interest, mandatory_tlb + mandatory_rcf, available_draw)`, with no absolute floor. Gross operands matter: a nearly zero net pre-financing cash flow can result from cancellation of material EBITDA and capex. This policy normalizes only roundoff at the feasibility boundary; it does not forgive a material shortage. The workbook uses its own cell formula, not a reference-code call.

The strict comparison is tested at adjacent binary64 values: set debt365, available draw0, and cash interest rate to the predecessor of1e-12, exactly1e-12, or its successor. Interest is `365*rate`, EBITDA/capex cancel, and guard is `365*1e-12`. The expected decisions are ok, ok, fail. Separate tests straddle the guard by1% at all three currency scales. Adjacent binary64 input values need not survive XLSX's decimal serialization distinctly, so those exact one-ULP tests are Python arithmetic tests, not claims of distinct spreadsheet cases.

Preferred keeps its existing relative1e-12 residual/proceeds rule. Additional tests put positive ordinary residuals at 0.5 and2 times this boundary across all three scales: the first becomes zero with null management IRR; the second remains positive with defined IRR. No widening to grader tolerance is appropriate. A large relative error in a tiny residual can otherwise flip null versus numeric returns.

Grading remains relative1e-7/absolute1e-7. A tiny numerical field such as a funding shortage can therefore compare equal to zero under the published output tolerance. That does **not** authorize changing its exact status, failure period, financing-prefix length, or exit nulls. No demonstrated valid implementation rejection required changing grader tolerance. The business guards and output-comparison tolerances serve different purposes.

## Specified domain versus exercised coverage

The public specification allows all four profiles, horizons1–8, positive maturity buckets beyond the horizon, bounded fractional rates, nonnegative financial amounts/rates, changing working capital, positive/zero/negative exit value, ownership endpoints, fiscal stubs and insolvency. The fixed45 hidden inputs sample these features, not every value or combination. In particular, they do not exhaust all horizon/maturity/ownership endpoints, zero tax and100% tax combinations, near-zero entry equity, arbitrarily extreme growth/rates, every leap/calendar pattern, or either numerical guard's immediate neighborhood. Currency-scale hidden cases rescale the base; they did not independently establish branch invariance near a liquidity threshold. The separate diagnostics and tests address the identified branch gaps without claiming to enlarge the45-case scored set.

The previous words 'finite inputs' were insufficient: preferred rate 1e308 over two years raises overflow even with finite entry equity; a fiscal horizon extending beyond year 9999 raises a calendar error. The public domain now explicitly requires mutually supported dates (last fiscal end at least 1900-03-01 and all eight projected ends no later than 9999-12-31), finite representable arithmetic, and finite values for every non-null numeric output. Specified null returns remain valid. No arbitrary financial amount/rate cap was added. Out-of-domain error handling remains unscored, and the validators are not an exhaustive overflow preflight. Native Excel's solver and the extreme representable edges of the domain remain unqualified; no universal correctness claim follows from 46 cases.
